Where it works — and where it does not

A white opacifier for polyolefin packaging and moulded parts.

Suitable for

  • White packaging film, label film and lamination film
  • Blow moulded HDPE bottles and containers
  • Injection moulded housings and household goods
  • PP sheet and thermoformed trays
  • Masterbatch blends where white is the base colour

Not suitable for

  • Transparent or translucent film — TiO2 is an opacifier by design
  • Long-term outdoor exposure unless the grade is supplied with coated, weathering-stabilised TiO2
  • Food-contact surfaces unless the specific grade is approved for that use
  • High-tenacity raffia at high loading, where pigment cuts tensile strength
  • Colour-critical work without a colour match trial on your own line

Suggested addition rate by process

Frequently asked questions

Why rutile TiO2 and not anatase?

Rutile has a higher refractive index and scatters light more efficiently, so it reaches the same opacity at a lower loading. It is also more weather-resistant. Anatase is cheaper but rarely used in packaging for those reasons.

How much TiO2 ends up in the finished film?

The finished part carries roughly 60% of the masterbatch loading. Adding a 60% grade at 5% puts about 3% TiO2 into the film. Confirm the number with a gravimetric check on your own line.

Will white masterbatch make my film brittle?

At high loadings, yes. Titanium dioxide particles act as stress points and reduce impact and tear strength. Most film producers stay in the 2–10% range and compensate with a tougher resin grade or a small addition of impact modifier.

What does whiteness ≥97 mean in practice?

It is a reflectance measurement taken on a 2 mm injection-moulded plaque against a standard white reference. Higher numbers look brighter and less yellow. The test method and instrument are stated on the datasheet.

What is the minimum order and lead time?

One metric ton per grade, 7–15 day production lead time. Trial quantities can be combined with other grades on one pallet.
