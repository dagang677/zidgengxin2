Black masterbatch for HDPE pipe: carbon black content and dispersion

Two numbers decide whether your HDPE pipe will pass UV ageing and pressure tests: the carbon black content inside the masterbatch, and its dispersion rating. Everything else — colour, carrier, MFI — is secondary for pipe grades.

Updated 29 October 2026 · Masterbatch Co. technical notes

Carbon black content: 35–50% is the pipe range

Black masterbatch for HDPE pipe is normally specified at 35–50% carbon black by weight. The trade-off is direct:

35–40%2.5–3%0.9–1.2%AdequateLower — less filler loading
40–45%2.2–2.8%0.9–1.2%AdequateBalanced — the standard choice
45–50%2.0–2.5%0.9–1.2%AdequateHighest saving per kg of pipe
The total carbon black in the finished pipe should sit at 0.9–1.2% regardless of which masterbatch grade you pick. Masterbatch content in pipe above 1.5% starts to reduce hoop stress, and below 0.8% UV ageing tests fail.

Not all carbon black is the same

UV stability depends not only on the quantity but also on the type of carbon black. Pipes for outdoor installation specify N330, N550 or N660 channel black grades. Furnace blacks and lamp blacks do not perform as well in UV ageing even at the same loading.

The cheapest mistake buyers make is to compare masterbatch price per kilogram without checking which carbon black the supplier is using. A furnace-black grade is significantly cheaper but fails the 1000-hour UV test that most pipe standards require.

Dispersion rating is pressure-rating critical

Dispersion is a measure of how evenly the carbon black particles are distributed in the masterbatch. Most pipe producers specify a dispersion rating of 4 or better on the ISO 18559 scale. The rating matters because:

  • Poor dispersion creates local stress concentrations that reduce hoop stress
  • Visible black streaks or specks are usually dispersion failures, not carbon black issues
  • Dispersion above rating 4 normally requires twin-screw compounding — a single-screw grade will not reach it reliably

Ask the supplier for a micrograph of a pressed sample, not just a rating number. A rating of 5 on a sheet from a single-screw factory should be treated with scepticism.

Carrier resin: HDPE pipe grades need HDPE carrier

HDPE pipe lines use HDPE virgin resin. Black masterbatch for pipe must therefore use HDPE carrier, not LDPE or LLDPE. A LDPE-carrier black masterbatch in HDPE pipe reduces weld-line strength and can cause delamination in hydrostatic tests.

The MFI of the carrier is less critical than for film grades — pipe lines run at lower shear and dispersion is handled by the compounder, but a pipe-grade black masterbatch should still quote an MFI under test condition 190 °C / 21.6 kg, not 2.16 kg.

2% vs 2.5% addition rate — what actually changes

Many buyers ask whether they can run 2% instead of 2.5% to save money. The answer is yes, if your masterbatch is 45–50% carbon black and dispersion is rating 4+. The total CB in the pipe stays within the 0.9–1.2% range. But two things change:

  • Visual uniformity — a lower addition rate makes streaks more visible if dispersion is imperfect
  • Weld integrity — pipe welds rely on a black pigment to locate the seam; below 2% the weld becomes harder to inspect visually

Common questions

What happens if carbon black content is too high?

Above about 1.5% total CB in the finished pipe, hoop stress and elongation at break both drop. Pipe standards specify 0.9–1.2% for a reason.

Can I use black masterbatch for pipe in injection parts?

Usually yes. UV-stabilised black masterbatch is a good choice for injection parts that need outdoor performance. The addition rate is typically 2–3% in injection.

Why are some black masterbatches cheaper?

Most often because they use furnace black instead of channel black, because dispersion is rating 3 or below, or because they use LDPE carrier. Check the carbon black grade and dispersion before comparing price.

Does carbon black affect pipe density?

Carbon black is denser than HDPE (about 1.8 vs 0.96 g/cm³), so masterbatch density rises with CB content. A 50% CB HDPE-carrier grade will have a density around 1.4 g/cm³.
